How this was built

Roster. All 66 people listed under Faculty on the department's own staff page (maastrichtuniversity.nl), retrieved 25 November 2026. Postdocs, PhD candidates, junior lecturers, engineers and support staff are not included.

Publications. Career totals and the five most-cited works per person come from OpenAlex. Each person was matched to an OpenAlex author record by surname plus a Maastricht University affiliation, then checked against initials, research topics and institution. Three people could not be matched with confidence and appear with no publication data.

Two dimensions. Each person is placed on two independent axes. The research cluster is their primary methodology: Physical Intelligence; Security, Privacy & Distributed Systems; Computing Paradigms; Learning, Reasoning & Discovery; Responsible Data Science; Human-centered AI; Mathematical & Decision Sciences. The application areas — health & life sciences, planetary health & sustainability, education & learning, law/governance & policy, industry/agri-food & logistics — are cross-cutting tags, so someone with a methods-led profile can still show up in the domains they work in, and a person can carry more than one.

How placement works. OpenAlex assigns every publication a topic, subfield and field. Each person's weighted topic profile (log-scaled publication counts per topic) is scored against keyword definitions for each cluster and each application area. The highest-scoring cluster becomes their primary one; the shares for the runners-up are shown on their profile. An application tag is applied when that area accounts for at least 15% of the matched topic weight. Placement therefore describes the publication record, not formal department structure — the department's own research-line label is shown separately on every profile.

Research overviews. The short narrative on each profile was written from that person's OpenAlex topics and most-cited publications. It summarises the publication record and may not reflect current or unpublished work. The “Cluster” and “Application” lines under it list the topics that drove each placement.

Manual placements. Five people are placed by their departmental research line rather than by score: three whose OpenAlex topics are purely application-domain, with no methodology topic to score against, and two whose records are contaminated (flagged on their profiles). One secondary cluster is also set by hand (Visara Urovi: Security, Privacy & Distributed Systems), and is marked as such on her profile.

Known limitations

  • Author disambiguation in OpenAlex is automated. At least one record merges two researchers with the same name, and some records are split across duplicates — per-person figures can be too high or too low.
  • Large collaboration papers (hundreds of co-authors) inflate citation counts for anyone who has worked on one.
  • Career totals mix a 30-year record with a two-year one; compare like with like.
  • Publication counts include preprints, reports, book chapters and dissertations, not only peer-reviewed articles.
  • Individual caveats are flagged on the affected profiles.
